# What Is Drylining? A Complete Guide for Homeowners and Builders

Drylining has become the standard method for creating smooth interior walls and ceilings in modern construction. But what exactly is drylining, and why has it replaced traditional wet plastering in so many projects across the local area and the UK?

## What Is Drylining?

Drylining is the process of attaching plasterboard (also known as drywall or gypsum board) to walls, ceilings, or stud frames without using wet plaster. Instead of applying layers of wet plaster that need days to dry, drylining uses pre-manufactured boards that are cut to size, fixed in place, and finished with jointing compound.

The result is a smooth, paint-ready surface that can be completed in a fraction of the time traditional plastering requires.

## How Does Drylining Work?

The drylining process typically involves these steps:

1. **Site Preparation** — The area is cleared, and any existing damaged plaster or wallpaper is removed.
2. **Framing (if needed)** — Metal or timber stud frames are built to create new wall structures or ceiling drops.
3. **Board Fixing** — Plasterboard sheets are measured, cut, and fixed to the frame or existing wall using adhesive, screws, or drywall nails.
4. **Taping and Jointing** — Paper tape and jointing compound are applied to seams, corners, and screw holes to create an invisible finish.
5. **Sanding and Finishing** — Once dry, the joints are sanded smooth and the surface is ready for painting or wallpapering.

## Benefits of Drylining

- **Speed** — Rooms can be completed in days rather than weeks
- **Cleanliness** — No wet plaster means less mess and no drying time
- **Versatility** — Easy to install insulation, soundproofing, and fire-rated boards
- **Cost-effective** — Generally faster and cheaper than traditional plastering
- **Easy to modify** — Simple to cut openings for sockets, pipes, and fixtures
